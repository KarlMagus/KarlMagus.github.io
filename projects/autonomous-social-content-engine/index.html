LinkedIn Content Operations Platform

A Django publishing console that drafts bilingual LinkedIn posts and long-form articles, queues them for review, and handles comments, newsletters, and podcasts through official APIs.

FocusResearch-backed drafting, background scheduling, LinkedIn publishing, and bilingual media.
Core TechnologiesDjango · OpenAI · Django-Q2 · LinkedIn OAuth
Project TypeCustomer integration
Summary
Situation

Publishing thought leadership meant researching a topic, writing a post and a long article, recording audio, and answering comments — twice, for English and Arabic.

Task

Build one Django application that drafts that package, queues it, publishes through official APIs, and engages without burst-posting.

Action

Implemented a research generator on OpenAI web search and structured outputs, a five-day Django-Q2 lookahead, LinkedIn OAuth, a three-step comment and reply pipeline, and dual-speaker text-to-speech.

Result

Operators review pre-generated drafts, publish to personal or company pages, and keep comments, newsletters, and podcasts on the same record. Rate limits and token checks sit in the queue, not in a promise of zero policy risk.

Product Surface

The product is a Django operations console for one publishing team, not a generic social network bot. The surfaces that shipped:

  • Posts: Category and subcategory prompts, OpenAI draft text, generated images, English or Arabic, and a choice of personal or company LinkedIn page.
  • Articles: A pluggable research generator that plans sections, charts, and diagrams, then stores bilingual article fields on the same record as the post.
  • Schedule: One-off scheduled prompts and weekly rules. A background worker pre-generates the next five days.
  • Engagement: Three sequential comments after publish, plus a reply view that answers comments on posts already sent.
  • Distribution: Gmail newsletter lists with signed unsubscribe links, dual-speaker podcast audio, and an analytics dashboard for published posts.

Research Pipeline

Long-form articles use ResearchV1Generator. It is an OpenAI Responses pipeline with structured outputs, not a five-agent theatre.

  • Web search: A research call with the web-search tool gathers notes and chart-ready figures for the topic.
  • Consulting-insights search: A search-term model queries an external insights index, then BeautifulSoup pulls article text for the planner.
  • Plan and write: Structured parsing produces a title and sections (including whether a chart or diagram is needed), then section, chart, and Mermaid calls fill the article. English and Arabic are both stored.
  • Where AutoGen fits: Some post-generation views use an AutoGen assistant as an optional helper. The research path itself is the Responses API.

Queue and LinkedIn Constraints

Scheduling is Django-Q2 against the application database, so the app does not need a separate Redis broker.

  • Five-day lookahead: A daily midnight job walks today plus four days, matches weekday rules, picks a subcategory, and generates text and an image. If a prompt already exists for that time and topic, it is skipped.
  • OAuth: Personal and company pages use LinkedIn OAuth. Access tokens last about 60 days; a health check warns operators before expiry and refreshes when a refresh token is available.
  • Replies: Mentioning someone in a reply requires a urn:li:person tag. The reply job records which comments were already answered and waits with jitter between calls so the account does not burst-reply.
  • Comments: After a successful publish, three comments go out in order: a deeper point from the post, a hook into a related article on the client CMS, and a call to action. The CMS is selected by the model from the client's blog API. The public write-up does not name that product.

Trade-offs

Choices that kept the deployment small:

  • Django-Q2 instead of Celery: The queue runs on the relational database. That avoids operating Redis for a single publishing app, at the cost of less isolation than a dedicated broker.
  • OpenAI TTS instead of a specialist voice vendor: Dual-speaker English and Arabic podcasts are stitched with PyDub from alternating TTS clips. The voices are consistent and priced per character; they are not a studio recording.
  • Approval before publish: Weekly automation prepares drafts. A person still approves what goes to LinkedIn. The system does not claim unattended posting is risk-free.
